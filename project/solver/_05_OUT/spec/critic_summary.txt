Convergence Behavior
The optimization converged in 128 of 200 allowed iterations, stopping due to a compliance plateau rather than the density-change tolerance (final_change = 0.0335, which exceeds the specified tol_change of 0.01; final_l2_change = 0.00202). Compliance dropped from 0.07411 to 0.01365, an 81.58% reduction, and the compliance-monotonicity check recorded zero violations, indicating a well-behaved descent throughout. Numerical convergence is therefore declared by the plateau criterion, not by the density-field stabilization criterion.

Engineering Anomalies
The primary anomaly is that the density-change stopping criterion (tol_change = 0.01) was NOT met at termination: final_change = 0.03351 is 3.35× above the threshold. The run stopped solely because compliance ceased to improve, while the density field was still evolving measurably. This dissociation suggests the optimizer may have been oscillating locally in density without further reducing compliance — a known SIMP/Heaviside behavior near the end of continuation. Additionally, the maximum density reached only 0.9874 (not 1.0), and the minimum is 0.0086 (not ~0.0), both slight deviations from crisp 0/1 bounds; at beta_final = 4.0 the Heaviside projection is relatively mild, which is consistent with this. The grey fraction of 0.199 (19.9%) is within the passing threshold of 0.35 but is non-negligible and warrants attention for manufacturing interpretation.

Physical Plausibility
For a 2-D cantilever beam (aspect ratio 1.6:1, fixed left edge, central point load at right edge, 40% volume fraction), an 81.58% compliance reduction and the reported final compliance of 0.01365 are physically plausible. The density bounds, volume fraction adherence (0.39999 vs. 0.4000 target), and monotone compliance history are all consistent with a correctly posed and executed SIMP problem. The validation status is passed across all four checks. The relatively high grey fraction (19.9%) at a low beta value is physically expected and does not by itself indicate a modeling error, though it limits direct manufacturing interpretability of the current density field.

Recommendation
Increase beta_final from 4.0 to at least 16–32 (via a more aggressive or extended Heaviside continuation schedule) and re-run; this will sharpen the 0/1 interface, reduce the grey fraction below 10%, and is likely to allow the density-change criterion (tol_change = 0.01) to be satisfied rather than relying on the compliance-plateau fallback — providing a more robust and manufacturable termination condition.
