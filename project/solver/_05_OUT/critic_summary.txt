Convergence Behavior:
The optimizer achieved a 73.1% compliance reduction over 100 iterations, which is a meaningful structural improvement, but the run did not converge — the final design change of 0.235 is substantially above any reasonable convergence threshold (typically < 0.01). The compliance appears to have continued evolving without settling into a stable topology, suggesting the optimizer was still actively redistributing material at termination.

Anomalies:
The grey fraction of 21.4% is within the passing threshold but is notably elevated for a cantilever problem at 100 iterations — well-resolved SIMP runs on this problem type typically achieve grey fractions below 10% by convergence. Combined with the non-convergence, this indicates the penalization (SIMP p-factor) may be insufficient to drive density toward binary values, or that the continuation schedule is too slow. The volume fraction landed precisely on target (0.496 vs. 0.500), which is correct behavior, but the large residual design change casts doubt on whether this is a stable equilibrium.

Physical Plausibility:
The compliance reduction and density bounds are physically reasonable for a cantilever under SIMP. The result is not implausible, but it is premature — the topology is likely still in transition and does not yet represent a fully resolved load-path structure with clear load-bearing members.

Recommendation:
Increase the maximum iteration count to at least 300 and implement or accelerate the penalty continuation schedule (e.g., ramp p from 1 to 3 over the first 60 iterations) to drive grey densities toward binary values earlier and allow the topology to stabilize before termination.
